I am an MSc student in Computer Science and Technology at Northwest University. My research interests lie in causal machine learning, causal representation learning, causal discovery, and multi-environment learning. I am particularly interested in learning stable and interpretable causal mechanisms from high-dimensional observations.

My previous work focused on medical image analysis, uncertainty-aware learning, multimodal learning, and generalization under distribution shifts. These experiences motivated me to study how causal structure and mechanism changes can support more reliable and transferable machine learning systems.

My current research explores causal representation learning under structured environment changes, especially settings where part of the variation across environments is driven by observable physical, biological, or temporal processes.

Research

Process-Informed Causal Representation Learning

Causal representation learning · Causal discovery · Multi-environment learning

I study how latent causal mechanisms can be learned when changes across environments are partly driven by structured, observable processes. The goal is to distinguish stable mechanisms from process-related and environment-specific variation, and to improve causal recovery and generalization across environments.

Robust Learning for Heterogeneous Medical Imaging

Uncertainty · Domain shift · Multimodal learning · Medical AI

My earlier research investigated uncertainty-guided curriculum learning, missing-modality classification, multimodal fusion, and medical image generation, with an emphasis on robustness to heterogeneous acquisition conditions and distribution shifts.
